2. Classification

Plant identity

  • Common name: Almond
  • Scientific name: Prunus dulcis
  • Family: Rosaceae
  • The edible almond is the seed/kernel of the fruit.

Major chemical constituents

α-Tocopherol Oleic acid Linoleic acid Phytosterols Polyphenols Amygdalin

Almond composition varies with cultivar, growing conditions and processing.

3. Vitamin E (α-Tocopherol)

Molecular formula: C29H50O2   |   Molar mass: 430.7 g/mol.

α-Tocopherol is the principal biologically active form of vitamin E. Vitamin E is a fat-soluble antioxidant that helps protect cells from oxidative damage.

4. Amygdalin — important clarification

Molecular formula: C20H27NO11   |   Molar mass: 457.4 g/mol.

Amygdalin is a cyanogenic glycoside found in some Prunus seeds. It is included here as a chemical constituent for study.

Safety note: amygdalin can release cyanide when metabolized. This page is for educational chemistry only; amygdalin should not be presented as a medicine or health supplement.

5. Functional groups

  • Vitamin E: phenolic –OH and an ether-containing chromanol ring; it also contains an aromatic ring and an alkyl side chain.
  • Oleic acid: carboxylic acid (–COOH) and alkene (C=C).
  • Linoleic acid: carboxylic acid (–COOH) and two C=C bonds.
  • Amygdalin: multiple hydroxyl groups, ether/acetal linkages and a nitrile (–C≡N) group.

6. Nutritional & biological significance

  • Almonds provide protein, dietary fibre, unsaturated fats, vitamin E and minerals such as magnesium.
  • Oleic and linoleic acids are important fatty acids in almond kernels; proportions vary by variety.
  • Almond skins contain numerous polyphenols and flavonoids.
